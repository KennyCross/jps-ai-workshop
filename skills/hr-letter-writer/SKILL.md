# HR letter writer

HR letters go out in the company's name and people keep them. They must be warm, clear, consistent and never promise anything HR hasn't agreed. This skill drafts them the same way every time.

## Before you write

1. Work out which letter is needed: acknowledgement, interview invitation, offer, regret or welcome. If it isn't clear, ask.
2. Collect the details the person has given. Anything not given becomes a placeholder.

## Rules that always apply

- **Never invent details.** Names, dates, times, salary, benefits, locations and managers' names are placeholders unless the person gave them: [Name], [Role], [Department], [Date], [Time], [Location], [Start date], [Supervisor], [Salary], [Contact].
- **Never guess about pay, benefits, leave or probation terms.** Write [CONFIRM: what is needed] instead.
- **Regret letters never give a reason** for the decision and never compare the person with other applicants.
- **Nothing about age, marital or family status, pregnancy, religion, health or disability** in any letter.
- **No personal data from the request.** If the person pastes an ID number, TRN, address, phone number or date of birth, leave it out and use a placeholder.
- **House tone:** warm, plain English, short paragraphs, no legal-sounding phrases such as "please be advised" or "herewith". Sign off as "[Name], Human Resources".

## Letter types

| Letter | Length | Must include |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Application acknowledgement | Under 100 words | Thanks, the role, what happens next and roughly when ([Date]) |
| Interview invitation | Under 150 words | Role, date, time, place or link, how long, who they'll meet ([Panel]), what to bring, how to confirm or ask for adjustments |
| Offer letter | Under 250 words | Role, department, [Start date], [Salary], [Supervisor], the conditions to meet before starting ([CONFIRM]), how to accept and by when |
| Regret letter | Under 120 words | Thanks, a clear statement that they were not selected this time, an invitation to apply for future roles. No reasons |
| Welcome email | Under 150 words | Start date and time, where to report, who to ask for, what to bring, what the first day looks like |

## After you write

Under the letter, add:

**HR to confirm before sending:** a bulleted list of every placeholder and [CONFIRM] item in the letter.

**Checks:** one line each, answered yes or no: no invented details; no reason given (regret letters); nothing about age, family, religion or health; within the length limit.

## Example

Request: "Regret letter for the CSR applicants we didn't pick."

Good opening: "Dear [Name], thank you for applying for the Customer Service Representative role and for the time you gave to the process. After careful consideration, we will not be offering you the role on this occasion."

Bad opening: "Dear Applicant, unfortunately other candidates had more call centre experience than you." This gives a reason and compares them with others.
