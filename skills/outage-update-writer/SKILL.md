# Outage update writer

Customers read outage messages when they are frustrated and worried. Every message must be accurate, calm and the same on every channel. This skill writes those messages from the internal outage note, and only from the note.

## Before you write

1. Find the internal outage note in the conversation, the attached files or the project files.
2. If there is no note, stop and ask for it. Never write an outage message from general knowledge.
3. List, for yourself, every fact in the note: areas affected, number of customers, cause, what crews are doing, restoration estimates per area, safety warnings, anything about bills or meter reading.

## Rules that always apply

- **Only facts from the note.** Never add a restoration time, date, cause, number, credit, refund or compensation that the note does not state. If customers will want something the note doesn't give, write "We don't have that yet" and say when the next update is coming if the note says so.
- **Safety message in every message, every channel:** "Treat every fallen line as live and report it to [Hotline]."
- **Keep placeholders exactly as written** in the note, such as [Parish A], [Hotline] or [REF]. Never replace them with real names or numbers.
- **No personal details.** Never include a customer's name, account number, address or phone number, even if one appears in the request.
- **Plain words.** No jargon such as "feeder", "restoration efforts", "assiduously" or "inconvenience caused". Short sentences.
- **One apology at most**, and always one clear next step for the customer.

## Formats

Write every channel the person asks for. If they don't say, write all four.

| Channel | Length | Must include |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SMS | Under 160 characters, including spaces | Area status, safety message |
| Social media post | Under 60 words | Area-by-area status, safety message, where to get updates |
| WhatsApp message | Under 80 words | Area-by-area status, safety message, what to do next |
| Agent call script | A 2-sentence opening, then short answers | Answers to "When will my power be back?" for each area in the note, "What do I do about a fallen line?" and "Will my bill be affected?" if the note covers bills |

## After you write: check and show your check

End every response with a short **Fact check** table:

| Statement in the messages | Where it is in the note |
| --- | --- |

List every factual statement you made and the line of the note it comes from. If any statement has no source, remove it from the messages before you answer. Then add a line **Not in the note:** listing questions customers are likely to ask that the note can't answer yet, so the team can ask for them.

## Example

Note says: "[Parish A]: main areas expected back by Tuesday evening. [Parish B]: no restoration estimate yet."

Good SMS: "Power update: main areas of [Parish A] expected back by Tue evening. [Parish B]: no time yet. Treat every fallen line as live; report to [Hotline]."

Bad SMS: "All customers will be back on by Wednesday and will get a bill credit." This invents a time for [Parish B] and a credit, and leaves out the safety message.
